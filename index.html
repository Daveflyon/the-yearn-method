The Yearn Method

A classroom hook for Miriam — Functional Skills, Landau

1. What This Is

Post-GCSE SEN learners at Landau don't need convincing that English and Maths matter. They already know. Knowing doesn't make them want to be there.

This tool is built around one line from Antoine de Saint-Exupéry: "If you want to build a ship, don't drum up the men to gather wood, divide the work, and give orders. Instead, teach them to yearn for the vast and endless sea."

The idea in one sentence: don't sell the subject. Sell the story of someone they already admire, and let the subject ride in on the back of it.

The mechanic is simple, repeatable, and takes two minutes at the start of a session:

StepWhat happens
1. QuoteMiriam reads out a quote or fact, doesn't say who it's from
2. GuessClass tries to guess who said it or who it's about
3. Hold the tensionIf no one gets it, she doesn't just tell them — she says "I'll tell you after the class"
4. RevealThe person is named. Learners lean in — this is the payoff moment
5. Teacher momentThe story of the teacher who made the difference for that person is told
6. Tie-inMiriam links it straight into today's lesson

Repeated across a term, this stops being a one-off trick and becomes Miriam's signature. Learners start asking "who's it today, Miss?" before she's even started.

2. Why It Works

Nobody is being told English or Maths matters. Nobody is being lectured. The hook is curiosity about a person they already like, and the lesson rides in afterwards, already earned.

Ordinary approachThe Yearn Method
"You'll need this for real life"Curiosity about someone famous first
Subject justified before it's taughtSubject arrives after the hook has already landed
Same message every weekNew person, same format, never stale
Passive listeningActive guessing, held tension, payoff
Why the pause matters: "I'll tell you after the class" is the yearn moment itself. Don't rush past it. The whole session, that answer is sat there unopened. That's the pull that carries them through the lesson.
3. How To Run It

Before the lesson

Open the generator below, pick a name (or let it choose one for you), read the reveal card once so you know the beats.

At the start of the lesson

BeatWhat to say / do
OpenRead the quote or fact cold. No name. Let it hang.
Guess"Anyone? Who said this?" Take two or three guesses.
If they get it"Well done — do you know how this helped them get where they are?"
If they don't"No problem. I'll tell you after the class." Move straight into the lesson.
Reveal (end or mid-lesson)Name the person. Let the reaction happen.
Teacher momentTell the real story — the teacher, the subject, what changed.
Tie-in"You never know where English/Maths takes you. Let's find out today — we're learning …"
Keep it light. This is not a lesson about the celebrity. Thirty seconds on the reveal, thirty seconds on the teacher moment, straight into the tie-in. If it starts to feel like a mini-lecture, it's gone on too long.

Variation keeps it alive

Don't reveal in the same spot every time. Sometimes at the start, sometimes mid-lesson, sometimes right at the end as a cliffhanger into next session. Predictable format, unpredictable timing.
